# Design direction — Flooring For All DBA Lil Man Big Van

## Direction
A cinematic, dimensional transportation-company website with a clearly separate, secondary flooring division. The first screen and roughly 70–80% of the service-specific content must make trucking unmistakably primary; do not lead with flooring or present both services as equal. Keep the site modern, commercial, friendly, and locally grounded. The competitor reference is a benchmark for ambition and 3D impact—not a template to copy.

## Design movement and core principles
Contemporary 3D editorial with local-service warmth and a commercial freight point of view. Make the first screen read immediately as a Winston-Salem transportation company: oversized type, deep tonal surfaces, a clearly disclosed yellow-box-truck concept image, layered depth, and prominent freight-quote and phone actions. Keep flooring visually and semantically separate later on the page. Maintain quick scanning, accessible contrast, lightweight movement, real details, and a calm, usable reading rhythm beyond the hero.

## Reference guardrails
The supplied reference demonstrates confidence through a dark cinematic stage, oversized headline, 3D transport/floor visuals and clear dual-service calls to action. Use those broad qualities only. Do not reproduce its exact layout, composition, graphics, text, colors or animation choreography. Avoid copying its logos, vehicle artwork, license/authority claims or any other unverified business details. Use the client's exact identity, service facts and contact information instead.

## Color philosophy
Use midnight evergreen/petrol (#0B292B) and deep charcoal (#101819) for cinematic sections; energetic chartreuse (#D9F27A) for route/light details and main action; warm persimmon (#F39A78) for selected highlights; and cool porcelain (#F5F4EF) for generous reading surfaces. Keep color solid, purposeful and high-contrast, not generic-gradient-heavy. This keeps a unique green/lime/coral brand signature rather than borrowing the competitor's black-and-gold palette.

## Layout paradigm
 A full-width midnight transportation hero with one primary “Get a Freight Quote” action, a direct freight phone action, and a yellow 26-foot box-truck concept render on the right. Identify it in visible copy as “Illustrative vehicle — not client fleet photo” until an actual client photo is supplied. Follow with Equipment, Freight Services, a transportation-only process, Winston-Salem/Triad service area, and the freight quote form, in that order. Then create a distinct visual break and introduce “Our Other Service — Flooring Services,” followed by the flooring services, the existing interactive LVP visualizer, and the separate free flooring-estimate form. Keep simple navigation and make the mobile layout a vertical sequence of confident, accessible panels rather than a squeezed desktop.

## Signature elements
- A thin route-progress rule and a small scrolling signal cue, both decorative and non-blocking.
- A clear equipment panel for the yellow 26-foot box truck and Sprinter van, without inventing fleet counts or ownership.
- Large transportation-first section markers, offset depth plates, route-map details and a visually separate flooring divider.
- Clear contact rail with the correct freight and flooring email/call destinations.
- A bespoke Triad schematic map labelled as illustrative and not to scale.

## Interaction and animation
Use short IntersectionObserver reveals and gentle pointer-based 3D tilt only where helpful. Never hide content if JavaScript is unavailable; do not animate continuously or hijack scroll. Mobile stays fluid with static, layered depth. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, keyboard focus, touch targets, semantic controls and clear menu/form states.

## Typography system
Use Space Grotesk for bold, slightly tight display text; DM Sans for readable copy and controls; and a restrained Georgia italic for one expressive phrase in selected headings. Favor oversized headings, short labels with useful hierarchy, and body copy with comfortable line length. Preserve legible sizes at small breakpoints.

## Brand essence and voice
Straight-talking, upbeat, capable and hands-on. Keep “Reliable. Local. Professional.” as the promise; make the experience feel human and easy to use. Transportation leads; flooring is the clearly signposted other service. No unsupported guarantees, fake testimonials, nationwide reach, exact miles/turnaround claims, fleet counts, credentials, or years-in-business claims.

## Wordmark/logo and signature color
Keep the user's real identity legible as live text: **Flooring For All** with **DBA Lil Man Big Van**; DBA means “doing business as.” Reuse the existing mark and favicon. The signature combination is deep evergreen, energetic chartreuse and warm persimmon on porcelain.

## Hero visual
Use one original 4:3 premium 3D concept render of a single, unbranded, bright-yellow 26-foot straight box truck in a clean commercial loading-yard setting. Keep the truck the unmistakable hero subject, with accurate vehicle proportions, polished materials, cinematic daylight, and no text or logos. The visible page must state “Illustrative vehicle — not client fleet photo” until the client provides an actual truck photograph. Do not use the existing generic white truck as the primary transportation image, and do not mix flooring into the transportation hero.


## Interactive flooring visualizer and estimator
Keep this tool within the existing flooring journey and visual identity. Render an illustrative room and perspective LVP plank surface in CSS; four keyboard-accessible finish-tone controls update the room's displayed floor. Label the tones as illustrative, not actual product samples. Provide editable room length/width in feet, an adjustable 0–20% material allowance, and an optional per-carton coverage input copied from the user's chosen product packaging. Show room area, area with allowance, and cartons only when the user supplies coverage. Do not invent a product catalog, product specifications, material/labor prices, or quote totals. Explain that the result is a planning aid, not a quote; let the user transfer the selected tone and dimensions into the existing flooring request form without submitting or opening email automatically. Keep all calculation local in the browser, responsive, accessible, and reduced-motion compatible.


## Customer reviews and project gallery content integrity
The site may present clearly labeled review and gallery sections even before proof materials are supplied. Do not invent testimonials, star ratings, review counts, or imply that existing illustrative/AI imagery is a completed customer job. Keep the empty states visible and intentional, and only replace them with customer-approved review quotes and real project photos with permission to publish. Feedback actions route to the matching existing flooring or freight business email.

## Transportation-first content and customer paths
Preserve the sequence: trucking/transportation → equipment → freight services → service area → freight quote → clear flooring-divider → flooring services → interactive flooring visualizer → free flooring estimate. Keep a prominent “Get a Freight Quote” action and a distinct, smaller “Flooring Estimate” path. Use the freight contacts for freight actions and the flooring contacts for flooring actions. Do not imply a specific number of vehicles; describe equipment options and load fit instead.
